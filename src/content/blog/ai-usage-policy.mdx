<ShortAnswer>
A useful AI usage policy fits on **one page**, uses **plain language**, and gives **real examples** from your business. It says which tools are approved, what information can and can't go in, when a person must check the output, and who to ask.
</ShortAnswer>

## Why most policies don't get read

They're too long, too legal or too vague. A ten-page document full of "users shall" gets signed and forgotten. People need something they can check in thirty seconds when they're about to paste something into a chatbot.

## What to put on the page

<NumberedList>
  <NumberedItem title="Approved tools.">Name them. Anything not on the list needs a quick check first.</NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="What can go in.">Your own drafts, public information, anonymised examples.</NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="What can't go in.">ID numbers, health or financial details, client data the tool isn't approved for, passwords.</NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="When a person must check.">Anything sent to a client, anything with numbers, anything legal or HR.</NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="Who to ask.">One named person, not "management".</NumberedItem>
</NumberedList>

## A starting template

| Section | Example wording |
| --- | --- |
| Approved tools | "You can use Microsoft Copilot with your work account." |
| Allowed | "Drafting emails, summarising your own notes, rewording documents." |
| Not allowed | "Client ID numbers, medical information or bank details, in any tool." |
| Always check | "Read every word before it goes to a client." |
| Questions | "Ask [name] before trying a new tool." |
