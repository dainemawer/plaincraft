<ShortAnswer>
Keep AI away from work that depends on **judgement**, **accountability** or **relationships**: hiring and firing, pricing strategy, difficult client conversations and anything where a person has to stand behind the decision. AI can help prepare for that work. It shouldn't do it.
</ShortAnswer>

## Judgement

AI produces plausible answers, which isn't the same as good judgement. When a decision weighs things that aren't written down anywhere, like a client's history, a team's morale or the risk you're willing to take, it needs a person who understands the context.

## Accountability

If something goes wrong, someone has to explain why the decision was made. "The AI suggested it" isn't an explanation a client, employee or regulator will accept. Hiring decisions are the clearest example: they affect people's livelihoods, and they need a person who can account for them.

## Relationships

Clients and staff can tell when a message was written to them and when it was generated for them. Use AI to draft routine replies. Write the difficult ones yourself.

> Use AI to prepare for the decision. Make the decision yourself.

## Where it still helps

<Checklist>
  <li>Summarising the background before a hard conversation.</li>
  <li>Drafting interview questions, then deciding with people.</li>
  <li>Pulling together the numbers before a pricing decision.</li>
</Checklist>

Part of every [readiness audit](/services/ai-readiness-audit) is a clear list of what to leave alone. Knowing where not to use AI is as useful as knowing where to use it.
