<ShortAnswer>
Most small-business quotes follow the same pattern: read the request, look up prices, apply the usual rules, write it up. AI can do the **reading and first draft** in minutes. A person still **checks the numbers and sends it**. The build usually runs on tools you already have.
</ShortAnswer>

## Where the afternoon goes

Watch someone prepare a quote and the time rarely goes on the thinking. It goes on finding the last similar quote, copying line items, checking the current price list, rewording the scope and fixing the formatting. That's exactly the kind of work AI does well.

## Step by step

<NumberedList>
  <NumberedItem title="Write down how quotes are built today.">The inputs, the price list, the rules ("travel over 50 km is charged"), and who signs off.</NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="Collect ten good past quotes.">These become the examples the AI follows for tone, structure and level of detail.</NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="Put the price list somewhere the tool can read it.">One current, tidy source. If prices live in three places, fix that first.</NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="Build the draft step.">The request goes in, a structured draft comes out with each line item linked to the price list.</NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="Keep a person in the loop.">They check quantities, prices and scope before anything reaches a client.</NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="Measure it.">Time per quote before and after, and how often the draft needs big changes.</NumberedItem>
</NumberedList>

## What to watch for

<Pitfalls>
  <Pitfall title="Messy pricing.">The AI will be as consistent as your price list, and no more.</Pitfall>
  <Pitfall title="Skipping the check.">A wrong price sent to a client costs more than the time saved.</Pitfall>
  <Pitfall title="Over-building.">Start with the common quotes. Leave the unusual ones to a person.</Pitfall>
</Pitfalls>
