## Start with the work, not the tool

Most owners I speak to start in the wrong place. They try a chatbot, get a mediocre answer and decide AI isn't for them. Or they buy licences for everyone and wait for something to happen.

A better question is simpler: _what does my team do every week that's repetitive, rule-based and boring?_ That list is where AI pays back. The tool comes second.

## The three jobs AI does well

Almost every useful AI project in a small business is a version of one of these.

<NumberedList>
  <NumberedItem title="It writes.">
    First drafts of quotes, proposals, emails and reports in your tone. A person checks and sends.
  </NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="It reads.">
    It pulls details out of invoices, contracts, CVs and forms, then puts them where they need to go.
  </NumberedItem>
  <NumberedItem title="It answers.">
    It replies to routine questions from customers or staff using your own price lists, policies and FAQs.
  </NumberedItem>
</NumberedList>

## What it costs to try

Less than most people expect. If you already use Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, you probably have access to capable AI tools today. The first project is usually about setting them up properly, not buying something new.

The real cost is time: someone has to describe the task clearly, test the output and decide what “good enough” looks like. That's the part most businesses skip.

> If a task can be explained to a new hire in five minutes, it's a good candidate for AI.

## A one-week plan to find your first win

<Schedule>
  <When label="Mon">Ask each person to list the three tasks they repeat most.</When>
  <When label="Tue">Circle the ones that involve writing, reading documents or answering questions.</When>
  <When label="Wed–Thu">Pick one. Try it with the AI tools you already have, using made-up data.</When>
  <When label="Fri">Compare: was it faster, and was it good enough? Decide whether to keep going.</When>
</Schedule>

If that week gives you a clear yes, you've found your first project. If it's a muddle, that's normal too, and it's exactly what [a readiness audit](/services/ai-readiness-audit) is for.
